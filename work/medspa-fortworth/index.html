The walkthrough

Five changes, and why each one books clients

Every change below is a business decision first and a design decision second.

Before

"Welcome to A Nurses Touch Skin Care & Med Spa in Fort Worth, Texas"

After

The headline says what they sell, not hello.

Nobody ever chose a medspa because a website welcomed them. "Skin care with a nurse's judgment" tells a first-time visitor the one thing competitors cannot copy: the person holding the syringe is a registered nurse. That single line does the selling the old paragraph never did.

The proof

Reviews and awards move to the top of the page.

Injectables are a trust purchase. A client deciding between two medspas picks the one whose 500+ five-star reviews and Fort Worth Magazine honors she saw before she scrolled. On the old site, the newest proof shown is a 2023 badge near the footer.

"Best of 2023" badge, halfway down a long page, last updated three award cycles ago.
The team

Before: five first names in a bullet list.
After: Daisy, the oncology nurse who opened a medspa.

Clients do not book "Marlow: Esthetician, Laser tech, Electrologist, Scalp Pigmentation Artist." They book a person they trust. Daisy's real story, critical care and oncology nursing before aesthetics, is the most persuasive paragraph this business owns, and the old site never tells it.

Before

"Book online" opens na02.patientnow.com, a different site with a different look.

After

Booking never leaves the page.

The phone number sits in the header on every screen, tappable, and the booking button stays inside the same design. Every step between "I want this" and "I booked" loses clients who were already convinced. The old flow made a warm lead start over on a stranger's website.

Before

"We are a team of specialitlty trained Nurses" · Suite 204 in one place, Suite 211 in another.

After

Clean copy, one verified address.

A medspa sells precision: steady hands, exact units, careful measurements. A typo on the team page and two different suite numbers tell the opposite story. The concept fixes both, because details are the product here.
