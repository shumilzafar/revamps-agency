What changed, and why it wins patients

Every change below maps to a real weakness on their current site. Nothing decorative, everything earns its place.

01

Booking moves front and center

Was: a "Request Appointment" form. A patient fills it out and waits for a callback, only during office hours.

Now: a real booking flow in the hero and the nav. Pick a service, pick a time, done in under a minute.

This is the money change. Implant and veneer patients research at 10pm. Every extra step between "I want this" and "I'm booked" is a patient choosing the practice down the road that lets them book online.

02

The 5.0 rating stops hiding in the footer

Was: 274 five-star reviews, buried at the bottom of the page where nobody scrolls.

Now: rating, stars and review count sit directly under the hero, next to the booking button.

Trust has to arrive before the ask. A new patient deciding in eight seconds sees the proof at the exact moment they decide.

03

The doctors get a face

Was: generic template illustrations. The practice is named after two real dentists patients never see.

Now: Dr. Thomas Karr and Dr. Clint Hardee, photographed, named, introduced. Their real team photo leads the page.

People choose dentists, not dental offices. When the name on the door matches the face on the site, the practice feels accountable and personal.

04

Services written for humans

Was: a wall of FAQ text written for search engines, not for a nervous patient.

Now: cleanings, veneers, whitening, implants, crowns, Invisalign, children's dentistry and emergency care, each in one plain sentence.

A patient with a chipped tooth should find "we fix that" in seconds, not wade through keyword paragraphs.

05

One real review beats ten polished claims

Was: marketing adjectives about "gentle, personalized care" with no patient voice.

Now: a single verbatim Google review from a real patient, with her name on it. Nothing invented.

Patients trust patients. One specific story ("he transformed my smile") outperforms a page of adjectives.

06

Hours and location in one glance

Was: hours scattered across pages. A patient on mobile should not hunt for "are you open Friday?"

Now: Mon 8 to 5, Tue to Thu 7 to 6, Fri 8 to 12. Address, map link and click-to-call phone in one block.

Half of dental searches happen on a phone, often with a toothache. Friction here is not a design flaw, it is a lost patient.
