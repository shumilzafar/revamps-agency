The calm approach

Built for people who dread the dentist

These are not slogans. They are how this office actually runs, described by Dr. McDougall himself in an interview with Dental Economics.

We listen first

Dr. McDougall practices reflective listening: he listens to what you say, then repeats it back, so you know for certain you were heard. Many patients arrive braced for a lecture. Here the first appointment starts with a conversation.

You stay in control

Raise a hand and everything stops, no questions asked. His assistant keeps a quiet dialogue with you through every procedure and warns you before you feel anything, even pressure. Patients who once needed sedation have graduated from it entirely.

Comfort is the vocabulary

The word "pain" is barely used in this office, on purpose. The team talks about comfort instead, because the words in the room shape how the visit feels. Small choice, enormous difference for an anxious patient.

Why this wins patients

Every change, and the reason behind it

Nothing here is decoration. Each decision answers a question a nervous patient is asking before they ever call.

Positioning

Lead with the fear, not the services

Dental anxiety is the highest-value patient segment: these are people who have avoided care for years and need the most work. The psychology license is the one story no Tulsa competitor can copy, and the old site buried it. The rebuild opens with it in the first sentence.

Trust

One voice, one set of hours

The current page shows two different hours and misspells the doctor's name. A patient deciding whether to trust you with their mouth notices. The rebuild has one consistent schedule, the name spelled right everywhere, and no stray links.

Proof

Real evidence, attributed honestly

No invented star ratings, no fake testimonials. The #1 Tulsa ranking from discovertulsa.net and the Dental Economics interview are real, recent, and verifiable, and both are named as sources. Real proof converts. Fabricated proof destroys trust the moment anyone checks.

Design

A visual system that feels like the promise

Deep spruce, warm paper, soft sage: colors that lower a heart rate instead of the usual clinical blue. Editorial serif type that reads like a calm conversation. If the site feels anxious, the anxious patient leaves.

Conversion

Every path ends at the phone

The phone number is tappable everywhere, the email is a real mailto link, and the request form confirms instead of vanishing into the void. Friction is where appointments go to die. The old site's "Request Appointment" was a dead end with no feedback.

Ready when you are.

No lecture, no rush, no judgment. Just a conversation about your teeth, with someone trained to understand the rest of you too.
